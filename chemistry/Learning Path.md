# Chemistry Learning Path

This course starts with basic ideas and builds toward everyday things such as water, metals, cooking, and fire. Do not rush ahead just to finish a list. The aim is to understand each idea well enough to explain it in your own words.

## Part 1: The ground floor
1. [[Chemistry/Matter|Matter]]: What counts as matter?
2. [[Chemistry/Energy|Energy]]: What is energy, and how can it change?
3. [[Chemistry/Interactions|Interactions]]: How can particles attract or repel?

**Checkpoint:** Explain the difference between matter, energy, and an interaction without memorizing a textbook sentence.

## Part 2: Inside an atom
4. [[Chemistry/Atoms|Atoms]]
5. [[Chemistry/Nucleus|The nucleus]]
6. [[Chemistry/Proton|Protons]]
7. [[Chemistry/Neutron|Neutrons]]
8. [[Chemistry/Electron|Electrons]]
9. [[Chemistry/Electron Cloud|Electron clouds and probability]]

**Checkpoint:** Explain what makes one element different from another, and why electrons are not tiny planets following neat circular tracks.

## Part 3: Why matter holds together
10. [[Chemistry/Atoms#Why atoms can bond|Why atoms can bond]]
11. [[Chemistry/Chemical Bonds|Covalent, ionic, and metallic bonding]]

**Checkpoint:** Explain why a chemical bond is not a physical string, and why forming a bond can release energy while breaking it requires energy.

## Part 4: From particles to everyday matter
Upcoming lessons:
- Why materials are solid, liquid, or gas
- Temperature, thermal motion, and particle attraction
- Why water behaves unusually
- Why fire burns and why some reactions need a starting push
- Why reactions can be energetically favorable but still happen slowly

These are planned topics, not lessons completed yet.

## How each lesson should work
For every new idea, ask:
1. What does the word mean?
2. What is happening at the particle level?
3. Why does it happen?
4. What everyday example makes it easier to picture?
5. What is the limit of the analogy?
6. Can I explain it in my own words?

## Questions to revisit
- What does it mean for electrons to be described by a probability cloud?
- Why does the combined arrangement of two atoms sometimes have lower energy?
- How do we compare the energy of a whole reaction, rather than looking at one bond alone?
