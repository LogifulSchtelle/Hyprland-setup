# Electron Cloud

## What does “electron cloud” mean?
An **electron cloud** is a way of picturing the regions around an atomic nucleus where an electron is more likely to be detected. It is not physical fog, and the electron is not a tiny planet following a neat circular path.

The cloud represents a **probability distribution**. Probability means how likely an outcome is. Quantum mechanics lets scientists calculate the likelihood of detecting an electron in different places.

## Why is this useful?
The arrangement and energy of electrons strongly influence how atoms bond and how they respond to light, electricity, and other substances.

## What happens when two atoms approach?
The electrons are influenced by both nuclei, so the allowed quantum arrangement of the combined system can differ from the arrangement in isolated atoms. In a hydrogen molecule, the two electrons occupy a shared molecular arrangement influenced by both nuclei.

This is **covalent bonding**. “Shared” does not mean the electrons sit still between the nuclei or shuttle back and forth like little balls. It means the quantum arrangement extends across the bonded atoms.

That arrangement can help keep the nuclei at a stable separation. See [[Chemical Bonds]].
