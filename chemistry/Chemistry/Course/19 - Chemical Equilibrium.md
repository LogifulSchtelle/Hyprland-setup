# Chemical Equilibrium

Many reactions can proceed in both directions. Equilibrium occurs when forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, so concentrations remain constant over time in a closed system at equilibrium.

## Dynamic, not frozen

For A ⇌ B, A can still convert to B while B converts to A. At equilibrium, the two rates match. Molecules do not stop reacting. Concentrations are constant, but they are not necessarily equal.

The equilibrium composition depends on temperature and the reaction's thermodynamic properties. Starting with more reactant or more product can lead to the same equilibrium state under the same conditions, if the system can reach it.

## The equilibrium constant

For:
\[
aA+bB \rightleftharpoons cC+dD
\]
the idealized concentration expression is:
\[
K_c=\frac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}
\]
For a rigorous thermodynamic equilibrium constant, activities are used. Pure solids and pure liquids are omitted from the usual equilibrium expression because their activities are treated as constant under the stated conditions.

A large K means products are favored at equilibrium relative to reactants under the defined standard-state convention; a small K means reactants are favored. K does not tell us how fast equilibrium is reached.

## Reaction quotient Q

Q has the same form as K but uses current concentrations or activities, not necessarily equilibrium values.
- Q < K: net reaction tends forward toward products.
- Q > K: net reaction tends in reverse.
- Q = K: the system is at equilibrium.

This is a powerful way to determine the direction of net change without guessing from the formula alone.

## Le Châtelier's principle

When an equilibrium system is disturbed, its composition shifts in a direction that tends to counter the disturbance. This is a useful prediction rule, not a substitute for thermodynamics.

- Adding a reactant often shifts composition toward products.
- Removing a product often favors further product formation.
- Changing pressure can matter for gas equilibria when the total gas stoichiometry differs between sides.
- Changing temperature changes K because it changes the thermodynamic balance.

A catalyst speeds both forward and reverse pathways and therefore does not shift the equilibrium composition. It helps the system reach equilibrium faster.

## Temperature and K

Unlike concentration or pressure changes at fixed temperature, temperature changes the equilibrium constant. For an exothermic forward reaction, raising temperature generally favors the reverse direction; for an endothermic forward reaction, raising temperature generally favors the forward direction, within the standard assumptions.

## Equilibrium is not completion

A reversible reaction can reach equilibrium with substantial reactant remaining. A reaction can be nearly complete if K is very large under the conditions, but “reversible” does not mean the reaction is exactly half done.

## Solubility equilibrium

A sparingly soluble salt can establish an equilibrium between its solid and dissolved ions. The solubility-product constant Ksp describes the equilibrium for that dissolution. If the ion product exceeds Ksp, precipitation is thermodynamically favored; if below, more solid can dissolve, subject to other conditions.
