# Energy, Heat, and Temperature

## Energy is a bookkeeping quantity

Energy is a measurable property of a system that helps track the capacity for change and the transfers that accompany change. It appears as kinetic energy, potential energy, chemical energy, thermal energy, electrical energy, radiation, and more. These are useful categories, not separate magical fluids.

**Kinetic energy** is associated with motion. **Potential energy** depends on a system's arrangement and interactions. A raised object has gravitational potential energy because of its position in Earth's gravitational field. Two charged particles have electric potential energy depending on their separation and charges. Chemical energy is largely associated with the electronic structure and interactions of atoms and molecules.

The total energy of an isolated system is conserved: it can transfer between forms but does not simply vanish. The practical task is to define the system and count transfers.

## Heat is not the same as temperature

**Temperature** describes a thermal condition related to how energy is distributed among microscopic degrees of freedom. In a simple ideal gas, absolute temperature is proportional to the average translational kinetic energy per particle.

**Heat** is energy transferred between systems because of a temperature difference. A hot object does not literally contain a substance called heat. It has internal energy; energy flows as heat when conditions permit.

**Thermal energy** is a less precise everyday term often used for energy associated with microscopic motion and interactions. In careful thermodynamics, distinguish a system's internal energy from energy transferred as heat.

## Why temperature and energy can differ

A bathtub of warm water can contain more total internal energy than a small cup of hotter water because it contains far more particles. Temperature is not the total amount of energy in the sample.

Adding heat can:
- increase temperature;
- change a substance's phase;
- drive a chemical reaction;
- be balanced by work or other energy transfers.

The result depends on the substance and conditions.

## Specific heat capacity

The heat needed to change temperature depends on mass and material. For a temperature change with no phase transition, we often use:

\[
q = mc\Delta T
\]

- q: energy transferred as heat (J)
- m: mass (g or kg, consistent with c)
- c: specific heat capacity
- ΔT: final temperature minus initial temperature

If 100 g of water warms by 5 °C and c ≈ 4.18 J/(g·°C), then q ≈ 100 × 4.18 × 5 = 2090 J. The equation assumes c is approximately constant over that temperature range and no phase change occurs.

Water's high specific heat means a relatively large amount of energy is required to change its temperature. This influences climate, cooking, and living organisms.

## Phase changes and latent heat

When ice melts at its melting point, added energy can increase the potential energy associated with the arrangement of water molecules rather than immediately raising temperature. The energy helps the solid structure lose its long-range order. During an idealized phase change at constant pressure, temperature can remain nearly constant while the phase changes.

A simple model is q = mL, where L is the latent heat for the relevant phase change. The value depends on the substance and transition.

## Exothermic and endothermic processes

An **exothermic** process transfers energy from the system to the surroundings, often warming them. An **endothermic** process takes in energy from the surroundings, often cooling them.

These terms describe the direction of energy transfer, not whether a process is fast or slow. A reaction can be exothermic but slow because it has a large activation barrier. A process can absorb heat and still occur spontaneously under the right conditions because entropy and other energy terms matter.

For reactions at constant pressure, chemists often use enthalpy change, ΔH:
- ΔH < 0: exothermic under the stated conditions.
- ΔH > 0: endothermic under the stated conditions.

Do not say “breaking bonds releases energy.” Breaking a bond requires energy input. Bond formation releases energy. A reaction's net energy change depends on the balance between bonds broken and bonds formed, plus the full system's changes.

## Work and energy transfer

Work is energy transferred when a force acts through a displacement or, more generally, through an organized macroscopic process. A gas expanding against pressure can do work on its surroundings. Electrical work can transfer energy through moving charge.

The first law of thermodynamics is an energy accounting rule. One common chemistry sign convention is:

\[
\Delta U = q + w
\]

Here ΔU is the change in internal energy, q is heat transferred into the system, and w is work done on the system. Other fields sometimes use different sign conventions, so always define the convention.
