# Acids, Bases, and pH

Acids and bases are not merely “strong” and “weak” liquids. The terms describe ways substances behave in reactions, especially reactions involving proton transfer.

## Brønsted-Lowry model

A **Brønsted-Lowry acid** donates a proton (H⁺) to another species. A **Brønsted-Lowry base** accepts a proton. In chemistry, H⁺ is a hydrogen nucleus, not a free ordinary hydrogen atom.

For example:
\[
HCl + H_2O \rightarrow H_3O^+ + Cl^-
\]
Hydrogen chloride donates a proton to water. Water accepts it and becomes hydronium, H₃O⁺. In aqueous solution, writing H₃O⁺ is usually more realistic than imagining bare H⁺ floating alone.

Water can act as either acid or base depending on its reaction partner. A substance that can do both is amphiprotic.

## Conjugate acid-base pairs

When an acid donates a proton, it becomes its **conjugate base**. When a base accepts a proton, it becomes its **conjugate acid**. The pair differs by one proton.

In HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻:
- HCl / Cl⁻ is one conjugate pair;
- H₂O / H₃O⁺ is the other.

## Strong versus weak

A strong acid ionizes extensively in water under the stated conditions; a weak acid ionizes only partially. Strength is not the same as concentration. A dilute strong acid can contain fewer acid particles per litre than a concentrated weak acid.

Strong/weak describes the extent of reaction or ionization, while concentrated/dilute describes the amount per volume.

## Water's ionization and pH

Water can transfer a proton between molecules:
\[
2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-
\]
At 25 °C, the ionic product is approximately:
\[
K_w=[H_3O^+][OH^-]=1.0\times10^{-14}
\]
Square brackets mean molar concentration in the equilibrium expression. The value changes with temperature.

pH is defined as:
\[
\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10} a_{H^+}
\]
In dilute introductory problems, activity a is often approximated by the numerical concentration relative to the standard concentration, giving pH ≈ −log₁₀[H₃O⁺]. More concentrated solutions need the more careful activity definition.

At 25 °C, neutral water has pH about 7. A lower pH indicates greater hydrogen-ion activity; a higher pH indicates lower hydrogen-ion activity and, in water at 25 °C, a more basic solution. Neutral does not mean pH 7 at every temperature.

## Logarithms and pH

Because pH is logarithmic, a one-unit decrease corresponds to a tenfold increase in hydrogen-ion activity. A change from pH 5 to pH 3 represents about a hundredfold increase, not merely “two units more acidic.”

For a simple solution with [H₃O⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L, pH ≈ 3.00.

## Neutralization and buffers

An acid and base can react to form products such as water and a salt. For hydronium and hydroxide:
\[
H_3O^+ + OH^- \rightarrow 2H_2O
\]

A **buffer** resists large pH changes when modest amounts of acid or base are added. A common buffer contains a weak acid and its conjugate base. It works because the pair can consume added base or acid, respectively, until the buffer capacity is exceeded.

## Safety

Never taste a chemical to identify it. Strong acids and bases can cause serious burns, and dilution can release substantial heat. In a laboratory, use the required protective equipment and follow local procedures. A familiar household label does not guarantee that a concentrated product is safe.
