# Master Review and Worked Problems

Use this file after studying the lessons in order. Try each question before reading its solution. The goal is to connect ideas, not merely remember labels.

## Part A: Foundations

### 1. Matter and classification
A sample contains only H₂O molecules and has no dissolved substances. Is it an element, compound, or mixture?

**Solution:** A pure compound. Each molecule contains hydrogen and oxygen chemically combined in a definite ratio. It is not an element because it contains more than one element.

### 2. Density
A sample has mass 45 g and volume 15 cm³. Find its density.

**Solution:** ρ = m/V = 45 g / 15 cm³ = 3 g/cm³.

### 3. Atom identity
A neutral atom has 8 protons, 8 neutrons, and 8 electrons. Identify the element and mass number.

**Solution:** Eight protons means oxygen. Mass number A = Z + N = 8 + 8 = 16, so this is oxygen-16.

### 4. Ion charge
A particle has 12 protons and 10 electrons. What is its charge?

**Solution:** Net charge in units of e is 12 − 10 = +2. The element is magnesium because it has 12 protons, so the ion is Mg²⁺.

## Part B: Bonding and structure

### 5. Shared electrons
What does sharing electrons mean in a covalent bond?

**Solution:** The electrons are described by a quantum state whose electron density can extend across the bonded nuclei. It does not mean electrons sit still between atoms or shuttle along classical paths.

### 6. Molecular polarity
Why is CO₂ nonpolar while H₂O is polar?

**Solution:** CO₂ is linear, so the two equal C=O bond dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel. H₂O is bent, so the O–H bond dipoles do not cancel.

### 7. Intermolecular versus intramolecular
What must mostly be overcome to boil water, and what changes in a reaction that decomposes water?

**Solution:** Boiling separates water molecules from one another by overcoming intermolecular attractions. Decomposing water changes chemical connectivity and requires breaking/forming chemical bonds in a reaction pathway.

## Part C: Equations and amount

### 8. Balance
Balance: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃.

**Solution:** 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃. Both sides have 4 Al atoms and 6 O atoms.

### 9. Mole calculation
How many moles are in 29.22 g NaCl if M = 58.44 g/mol?

**Solution:** n = m/M = 29.22 / 58.44 = 0.500 mol.

### 10. Limiting reactant
For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, you have 5 mol H₂ and 2 mol O₂. Which limits?

**Solution:** Two moles O₂ require 4 mol H₂. Since 5 mol H₂ is available, O₂ is limiting; 1 mol H₂ remains in excess if the reaction proceeds fully.

## Part D: Energy and change

### 11. Heat calculation
How much heat is needed to warm 200 g of water by 3 °C, using c = 4.18 J/(g·°C)?

**Solution:** q = mcΔT = 200 × 4.18 × 3 = 2508 J, or about 2.51 kJ.

### 12. Thermodynamics versus kinetics
A reaction has ΔG < 0 but proceeds very slowly. Is that contradictory?

**Solution:** No. Negative ΔG indicates thermodynamic favorability under the stated conditions, while the rate may be low because the activation barrier is high or the mechanism is slow.

### 13. Equilibrium
A reaction has Q < K. Which direction is favored for net change?

**Solution:** Forward, toward products, until Q reaches K, assuming the reaction quotient is defined consistently and conditions remain fixed.

### 14. pH
In the simple dilute approximation, [H₃O⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L. Find pH.

**Solution:** pH ≈ −log₁₀(10⁻⁴) = 4.

## Part E: Explain, do not memorize

7. Why do solids, liquids, and gases behave differently even though all are made of particles?

## Final synthesis

Chemistry connects microscopic structure to observable behavior. Nuclear charge and quantum rules shape electron states. Electron states and electromagnetic interactions determine bonding. Bonding and molecular geometry determine how particles interact. Particle motion and interactions shape phases and properties. Reactions rearrange atoms, and stoichiometry counts them. Thermodynamics describes energetic favorability, kinetics describes rates, and equilibrium describes the composition toward which reversible reactions settle under specified conditions.

That chain is the backbone of the subject. Every specialized field adds detail to it.
