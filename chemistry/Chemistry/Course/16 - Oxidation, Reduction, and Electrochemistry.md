# Oxidation, Reduction, and Electrochemistry

Oxidation-reduction, or **redox**, reactions involve changes in oxidation states and often the transfer of electrons between chemical species.

## The basic pair

- **Oxidation:** loss of electrons in the electron-bookkeeping picture; oxidation state increases.
- **Reduction:** gain of electrons; oxidation state decreases.

They always occur together in an overall redox process because electrons lost by one species must be accounted for by another, or transferred through an external circuit.

Remember: OIL RIG is a mnemonic, not the explanation. Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

## Oxidation states

An oxidation state is a formal bookkeeping assignment, not necessarily the real electric charge on an atom. Rules help assign them:
- an element in its uncombined elemental form has oxidation state 0;
- a monatomic ion's oxidation state equals its charge;
- oxygen is usually −2 in compounds, with exceptions;
- hydrogen is usually +1 with nonmetals and −1 in metal hydrides;
- oxidation states sum to the overall charge.

In H₂O, each H is +1 and O is −2, giving a total of zero. In Mg²⁺, magnesium is +2.

## Example: magnesium and oxygen

\[
2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO
\]

Magnesium changes from oxidation state 0 to +2, so it is oxidized. Oxygen changes from 0 to −2, so it is reduced. The ionic model of MgO is helpful, though the real solid has a collective lattice.

The oxidizing agent causes oxidation by accepting electrons and is itself reduced. The reducing agent causes reduction by donating electrons and is itself oxidized.

## Half-reactions

A redox reaction can be separated conceptually into oxidation and reduction half-reactions. For example:
\[
Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-
\]
\[
Cu^{2+}+2e^- \rightarrow Cu
\]
Adding them cancels the electrons:
\[
Zn + Cu^{2+} \rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu
\]

The electrons are bookkeeping for charge transfer. In a galvanic cell, they can be directed through an external wire, allowing electrical work to be obtained.

## Batteries and cells

A **galvanic (voltaic) cell** uses a spontaneous redox reaction to drive electron flow through a circuit. Oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode. In a galvanic cell, the anode is negative and the cathode positive; in an electrolytic cell, external power drives a nonspontaneous reaction, so electrode signs differ. The anode is always where oxidation occurs; the cathode is always where reduction occurs.

An electrolyte and ion movement help maintain charge balance inside the cell. Electrons travel through the external circuit, not through the ordinary electrolyte solution as free metal-like current carriers.

## Corrosion

Rusting iron is a collection of electrochemical processes involving iron, oxygen, water, and ions. Moisture helps enable ionic movement, while oxygen participates in reduction. Salt can accelerate corrosion by increasing solution conductivity. Paint, coatings, galvanization, and suitable material choices reduce exposure or redirect the electrochemical process.

## Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a chemical change that would not proceed spontaneously under the same conditions. Applications include metal refining, metal plating, and producing chemicals. Product formation depends on electrode materials, electrolyte composition, concentration, and applied potential.
