# Chemical Bonds

## What is a chemical bond?
A **chemical bond** is a stable arrangement of atoms caused by their electric interactions and quantum behavior. It is not a tiny rope that ties atoms together.

## 1. Covalent bonding
In **covalent bonding**, atoms share electron density in a combined quantum arrangement. Electron density describes how the electron distribution is spread through space.

**Example:** Two hydrogen atoms can form a hydrogen molecule, H₂. The shared electron arrangement can lower the energy of the combined system and help hold the atoms at a stable distance.

## 2. Ionic bonding
An **ion** is an atom or group of atoms with a net electric charge because it has more or fewer electrons than protons.

In many ionic materials, positively and negatively charged ions attract and form an extended solid structure. Ordinary table salt, sodium chloride (NaCl), is a familiar example. A grain of salt is not made of separate little NaCl molecules lined up like beads; it is a repeating arrangement of sodium and chloride ions.

## 3. Metallic bonding
In a metal, some electrons are spread across the material rather than being associated with just one atom-to-atom pair. This helps explain why metals conduct electricity and why many can be bent or shaped without immediately shattering.

## A useful caution
These categories are models that help explain different bonding patterns. Real materials can have more complicated mixtures of bonding behavior.
