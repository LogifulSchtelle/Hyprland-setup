- Covalent bonding involves shared electron density.
- Ionic bonding involves attraction between charged ions, alongside the broader electronic structure of the material.
- Metallic bonding involves electrons distributed throughout a metal's structure.

A. Covalent bonding

Atoms share electron density in a way that helps hold them together.

Example: Hydrogen atoms forming hydrogen gas, H₂.

B. Ionic bonding

Positive and negative ions attract each other electrically.

An ion is an atom or group of atoms with an overall electric charge.

Example: The ions in ordinary table salt, sodium chloride.

C. Metallic bonding

In metals, electrons are distributed across the material rather than being confined to individual pairs of atoms.

This helps explain why metals conduct electricity and can often be shaped without immediately breaking apart.
