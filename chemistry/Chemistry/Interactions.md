# Interactions

## What is an interaction?
An **interaction** is a physical influence that makes parts of a system affect one another. Depending on the interaction, objects or particles may attract, repel, transfer energy, or change how they move.

A magnet is a familiar example, but chemistry mostly depends on electric interactions and the quantum rules governing electrons.

## Electric charge
Electric charge is a property of particles that determines how they respond to electric forces.
- Opposite charges attract.
- Like charges repel.
- A proton has positive charge.
- An electron has negative charge.
- A neutron has no net electric charge.

This helps explain why electrons are attracted to atomic nuclei, while electron-electron and nucleus-nucleus electric interactions tend to repel.

## Is that the whole story?
Not quite. **Quantum mechanics** is the set of rules scientists use to describe very small systems. It determines which electron states are possible and how electrons are distributed. The **strong nuclear interaction** also helps hold protons and neutrons together inside most nuclei.

For ordinary chemical bonding, electric interactions and quantum mechanics are the central starting points.
