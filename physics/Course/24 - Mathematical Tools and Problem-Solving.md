# 24 — Mathematical Tools and Problem-Solving

## Algebra expresses relationships
An equation states that two expressions have the same value. Rearranging it preserves equality when the same valid operation is applied to both sides. Solve symbolically before inserting numbers so the structure of the relationship remains visible.

## Scientific notation
Write very large or small values as \(a\times10^n\). Powers of ten make scale comparisons and unit conversions easier. Estimate the answer before calculating; implausible magnitude is a warning to recheck assumptions and arithmetic.

## Vectors and components
A vector has magnitude and direction. For a vector of magnitude \(A\) at angle \(\theta\) from the positive x-axis, \(A_x=A\cos\theta\) and \(A_y=A\sin\theta\). Add vectors by adding matching components; recover magnitude with \(A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}\).

## Graphs
Slope means change in the vertical quantity divided by change in the horizontal quantity. Position-time slope gives velocity; velocity-time slope gives acceleration. Area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement. A graph's meaning depends on its axes.

## Proportional reasoning
If \(y\propto x\), doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\). If \(y\propto1/x^2\), doubling \(x\) reduces \(y\) to one quarter. This often predicts the direction and scale of an answer before arithmetic.

## Calculus preview
A derivative describes an instantaneous rate of change: velocity is the derivative of position, acceleration the derivative of velocity. An integral accumulates contributions: area under a velocity-time curve gives displacement. The physical meaning can be understood before mastering the formal mathematics.

## Checklist
Draw and label; state assumptions; track vector directions; choose a governing principle; check dimensions; estimate the order of magnitude; then explain what the answer means and where the model could fail.
