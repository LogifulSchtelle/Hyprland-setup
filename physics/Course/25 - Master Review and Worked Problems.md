# 25 — Master Review and Worked Problems

## The core connections
- Motion describes change: position, velocity and acceleration.
- Forces explain changes in motion: \(\sum\vec F=m\vec a\) in Newtonian mechanics.
- Energy tracks transfers and transformations: \(W_{net}=\Delta K\).
- Momentum tracks motion through interactions: impulse changes momentum; isolated systems conserve total momentum.
- Fields describe interactions through space: gravitational, electric and magnetic.
- Waves transport patterns and energy: \(v=f\lambda\).
- Thermodynamics tracks energy and process direction: energy is conserved while entropy constrains spontaneous change.
- Relativity and quantum theory extend classical models in their respective domains.

## Worked problem 1: speed versus velocity
A person walks 100 m east in 50 s, then 40 m west in 20 s.
- Distance = 140 m; time = 70 s.
- Average speed = 140/70 = 2 m/s.
- Displacement = 60 m east.
- Average velocity = 60/70 ≈ 0.86 m/s east.

## Worked problem 2: net force
A 5 kg cart is pulled right with 20 N while friction acts left with 5 N. Net force is 15 N right, so \(a=F/m=15/5=3\,\text{m/s}^2\) right. The pulling force alone is not the net force.

## Worked problem 3: falling energy
A 1 kg object falls 2 m from rest, ignoring air resistance. Its gravitational potential energy decreases by \(mgh\approx19.6\) J, becoming kinetic energy. Thus \(\frac12mv^2=mgh\), giving \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\approx6.3\) m/s. Real air resistance transfers some energy to the surroundings.

## Worked problem 4: a resistor
A 12 V ideal battery connects to a 6 Ω resistor. Current is \(I=V/R=2\) A; power is \(P=VI=24\) W. Real batteries have internal resistance and can deliver a lower terminal voltage under load.

## Worked problem 5: a wave
A sound wave has frequency 500 Hz and speed 340 m/s. Wavelength is \(\lambda=v/f=0.68\) m. When sound enters another medium, frequency is generally set by the source while speed and wavelength may change.

## Misconceptions to retire
- A force is not needed to maintain constant velocity; net force changes velocity.
- Acceleration does not simply mean speeding up.
- Friction does not destroy energy; it transfers energy into other forms.
- Action-reaction forces act on different objects.
- Mass and weight differ.
- Heat is energy transfer, not a substance stored in an object.
- Current is not consumed by a resistor; energy is transferred.
- Gravity has not vanished in orbit.
- Quantum uncertainty is not merely faulty measurement.
- A scientific theory is an evidence-supported framework, not a guess.
