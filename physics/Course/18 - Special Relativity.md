# 18 — Special Relativity

## Two starting principles
The laws of physics have the same form in all inertial reference frames, and vacuum light speed is the same for all inertial observers. These principles conflict with everyday assumptions about absolute time, so space and time measurements must transform together.

## Simultaneity
Events simultaneous in one inertial frame need not be simultaneous in another. This is not merely a delay in seeing light; it is a feature of how different observers' space-time coordinates relate.

## Time dilation and length contraction
Define the Lorentz factor \(\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\). A moving clock is measured to tick more slowly: \(\Delta t=\gamma\Delta\tau\), where \(\Delta\tau\) is proper time measured in the clock's rest frame. Length along the direction of motion is contracted by \(1/\gamma\) compared with proper length.

These effects are tiny at everyday speeds but matter in particle experiments and satellite navigation.

## Mass and energy
Rest energy is \(E_0=mc^2\). This connects mass with energy, but it does not mean all an object's mass can be casually converted into usable energy. Energy and momentum are unified in relativity.

Special relativity describes inertial frames without gravity as curved spacetime. General relativity extends the picture to gravity.
