# 21 — Particle Physics and the Standard Model

## Layers of matter
Atoms contain electrons and nuclei. Nuclei contain protons and neutrons, which are composite particles built from quarks bound by gluons. Electrons are leptons and appear elementary in current experiments.

## Matter particles
The Standard Model organizes elementary matter particles into quarks and leptons across three generations. Ordinary stable matter mainly uses first-generation particles: up and down quarks in protons and neutrons, plus electrons. Heavier generations appear in high-energy processes and decay rapidly.

## Interaction carriers
- Photons mediate electromagnetic interactions.
- Gluons mediate the strong interaction.
- W and Z bosons mediate the weak interaction.
- The Higgs field is associated with the mechanism by which elementary particles acquire mass through their interactions with it.

Gravity is not included in the Standard Model as a complete quantum interaction. A confirmed quantum theory of gravity remains an open challenge.

## Antimatter
Many particles have antiparticles with the same mass and opposite additive quantum numbers. A particle and antiparticle can annihilate into other particles while conserving energy, momentum and other relevant quantities.

## Open questions
The Standard Model does not explain all observations and mysteries, including dark matter, dark energy, the cosmic matter-antimatter imbalance and quantum gravity. Neutrino masses also require physics beyond the original minimal model.
