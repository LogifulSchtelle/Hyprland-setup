# 17 — Light, Reflection, Refraction, and Lenses

## Light
Light is electromagnetic radiation. In vacuum it travels at \(c\approx3.00\times10^8\,\text{m/s}\), and \(c=f\lambda\). When light enters a stationary material, its frequency remains set by the source while speed and wavelength change.

## Reflection
The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, measured from the normal, a line perpendicular to the surface. Smooth surfaces form clear reflected images; rough surfaces scatter light in many directions.

## Refraction
Light changes direction when its speed changes at a boundary. Snell's law is \(n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2\), where refractive index \(n=c/v\) in the simple phase-speed description. Entering a higher-index medium bends the ray toward the normal, except for incidence exactly along the normal.

## Lenses
Converging lenses bring parallel rays toward a focus; diverging lenses spread them as if from a virtual focus. For a thin lens in the paraxial approximation, \(1/f=1/d_o+1/d_i\). A consistent sign convention is essential. Under a common convention, magnification is \(m=-d_i/d_o\).

## Wave effects
Diffraction is spreading around openings and obstacles, especially when their size is comparable to wavelength. Interference comes from overlapping waves. Polarization describes the orientation of the transverse electric-field oscillation.

## Checkpoint
Why does a straw look bent in water? When light enters glass, which change: frequency, speed, wavelength, or all three?
