# 13 — Temperature, Heat, and Thermodynamics

## Temperature is not the same as heat

Temperature characterizes a system's thermal state and, for simple gases, relates to the average random kinetic energy of particles. **Heat** is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. A hot object does not “contain heat” as a stored substance; it has internal energy.

Internal energy includes microscopic motion and interactions. Thermal energy transfer can occur by conduction, convection, or radiation.

## First law of thermodynamics

Using the convention that (W_{\text{by}}) is work done by the system:
(\Delta U=Q-W_{\text{by}}).
Energy entering as heat increases internal energy; work done by the system decreases it, all else equal. Other sign conventions exist, so always state which one is used.

## Specific heat and phase changes

For a temperature change without phase change, (Q=mc\Delta T), where (c) is specific heat capacity. During melting or boiling at a fixed pressure, energy can change the arrangement of particles without raising temperature; (Q=mL) models latent heat.

## Second law and entropy

The second law distinguishes possible process directions. Heat flows spontaneously from hotter to colder objects, not the reverse without other changes. In an isolated system, total entropy does not decrease. Entropy is not simply “disorder”; it tracks the number and distribution of microscopic possibilities consistent with a macroscopic state.

## Heat engines

A heat engine takes energy from a hot reservoir, converts some into work, and releases the remainder to a colder reservoir. No engine operating in a cycle can convert all absorbed heat into work. This limit is thermodynamic, not merely a flaw in engineering.
