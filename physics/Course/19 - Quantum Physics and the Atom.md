# 19 — Quantum Physics and the Atom

## Why quantum theory was needed
Classical physics could not fully explain blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect, and discrete atomic spectra. At small scales, nature is not well described by imagining every object as a tiny classical billiard ball.

## Quantized energy
Bound atoms can have only certain allowed energy states. A photon has energy \(E=hf\), where \(h\) is Planck's constant and \(f\) is frequency. Higher-frequency photons carry more energy. In the photoelectric effect, electrons are ejected only when individual photons provide sufficient energy; simply increasing the brightness of light below the threshold does not make each photon more energetic.

## Wave and particle behaviour
Quantum objects are described by states that produce wave-like interference and particle-like detection events. They are not merely classical particles alternating costumes. The de Broglie wavelength is \(\lambda=h/p\), where \(p\) is momentum.

## Probability and uncertainty
A wavefunction is a mathematical state used to calculate measurement probabilities. The uncertainty principle is not just bad equipment: position and momentum cannot both have arbitrarily narrow distributions. In one dimension, \(\Delta x\Delta p\geq\hbar/2\).

## Atoms
Electrons occupy quantum states around a compact nucleus. Atomic spectra arise when transitions between allowed energies absorb or emit photons. Orbitals are probability distributions, not planetary tracks.
