# 14 — Electric Charge and Electric Fields

## Charge is a property of matter

Electric charge comes in positive and negative forms. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Charge is conserved: ordinary processes move charge between objects rather than creating net charge from nothing.

## Coulomb's law

For point charges separated by distance (r):
(F=k\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}).
The force acts along the line joining them. The sign of the charges tells whether the interaction is attractive or repulsive; the formula above gives magnitude.

## Fields explain influence throughout space

The electric field is force per unit positive test charge: (\vec E=\vec F/q). A field assigns a vector to each location, allowing us to describe what force a charge would experience there. For a point charge, field magnitude scales as (E=k|Q|/r^2).

Field lines are visual tools, not physical strings. Their direction shows the force direction on a positive test charge; density represents field strength qualitatively.

## Conductors and insulators

In conductors, some charges can move relatively freely. In insulators, charges are more tightly bound. Charging by friction transfers electrons; induction redistributes charge without direct contact and can produce a net charge when grounding is involved.

## Electric potential energy

Moving charge in a field can change electric potential energy. Electric potential is potential energy per charge, measured in volts. Voltage differences become central in circuits because they describe energy transferred per unit charge.
