# 12 — Oscillations, Waves, and Sound

## Oscillation and restoring influence

An oscillation repeats around an equilibrium. A spring-mass system oscillates because displacement creates a restoring force. For an ideal mass–spring system, angular frequency is \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\) and period is (T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}\).

## What is a wave?

A wave is a traveling pattern that carries energy and momentum. In many waves, the material oscillates locally rather than travelling all the way with the pattern. Water waves are more complex than a simple up-and-down line, but the idea remains useful.

- **Amplitude:** maximum disturbance.
- **Wavelength** \(\lambda\): distance between matching points in successive cycles.
- **Frequency** (f): cycles per second, measured in hertz.
- **Period** (T=1/f): time for one cycle.
- **Wave speed:** (v=f\lambda).

Frequency is set by the source; speed depends on the medium and conditions, so wavelength changes when wave speed changes.

## Sound

Sound is a mechanical pressure wave and needs a medium. In air, it is usually longitudinal: air parcels oscillate mainly along the direction the wave travels. Pitch is related to frequency; loudness relates to intensity and perception, not amplitude alone in every context.

Sound travels faster in many solids than in gases because wave speed depends on both elasticity and density. It cannot travel through a perfect vacuum.

## Interference and resonance

When waves overlap, their disturbances add (superposition). They can reinforce or cancel. Resonance occurs when periodic driving matches a system's natural frequency closely enough to produce a large response, limited by damping and nonlinear effects.
