# 23 — Physics of Materials and Everyday Technology

## Why materials differ
Material properties emerge from atoms, bonding, arrangement, defects and temperature. Chemistry explains much about bonding; physics helps explain collective behaviour when enormous numbers of atoms interact.

## Conductors, insulators and semiconductors
In solids, electrons occupy allowed energy bands. Conductors have available states that allow charge to move readily. Insulators have a large energy gap that makes ordinary conduction difficult. Semiconductors have a smaller gap, and their conductivity can be controlled by temperature, light, impurities (doping) and electric fields.

## Heat and expansion
Thermal conductivity describes how readily heat flows through a material. Specific heat capacity describes energy needed per mass per degree of temperature change. Thermal expansion occurs because average atomic spacing changes with temperature; different expansion rates can stress joints or make a bimetal strip bend.

## LEDs and solar cells
An LED emits light when electrons and holes recombine across a semiconductor energy gap. The gap helps determine photon energy and colour. A solar cell absorbs photons to create mobile charge carriers; its internal fields and structure help separate charges to produce electrical power.

## Sensors and computing
Accelerometers measure motion using tiny mechanical structures or other physical effects. Microphones convert pressure variations into electrical signals. Camera pixels convert photons into electrical signals. Computers rely on controlled switching in transistors, with resistance, capacitance, heat and quantum effects influencing performance.

## Efficiency
Efficiency compares useful output with total input, using a clearly defined system boundary. Real devices transfer some energy into less useful forms, often heat. Engineering improves how energy is transferred and managed rather than making energy disappear.
