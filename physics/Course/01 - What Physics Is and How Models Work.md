# 01 — What Physics Is and How Models Work

## Physics studies patterns, not isolated facts

A dropped mug, an orbiting Moon, a guitar string, and a phone's accelerometer seem unrelated. Physics searches for common patterns beneath them: motion, interaction, energy transfer, waves, and fields.

A **physical quantity** is a property that can be defined and measured, such as length, elapsed time, mass, temperature, or electric current. A **law** summarizes a reliable relationship. A **theory** connects many laws and observations into a tested explanatory framework. In science, a theory is not a casual guess.

## The model cycle

1. Observe a pattern.
2. Propose a model with explicit assumptions.
3. Derive a prediction.
4. Compare the prediction with measurement.
5. revise the model if evidence disagrees.

A model is useful within a domain. Newtonian mechanics predicts everyday motion extremely well, but very high speeds require relativity and very small systems require quantum physics. The older model remains useful where its assumptions are good approximations.

## Measurement and uncertainty

No measurement has infinite precision. Instruments have limits; repeated measurements vary; methods can introduce systematic bias. A responsible result reports suitable precision and, when important, uncertainty.

**Accuracy** is closeness to the accepted value. **Precision** is repeatability or resolution. A ruler can give very repeatable readings and still be miscalibrated.

## Correlation and causation

Two quantities changing together does not prove one causes the other. A causal claim needs a mechanism and evidence that competing explanations have been addressed.

## Checkpoint

- Why is a theory stronger than a single observation?
- When is a simplified model better than a detailed one?
- What evidence would make you abandon a favorite explanation?
