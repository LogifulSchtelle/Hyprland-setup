# 15 — Voltage, Current, Resistance, and Circuits

## Current is charge flow

Electric current is charge passing a cross-section per time: (I=\Delta Q/\Delta t), measured in amperes. Conventional current points in the direction positive charge would move; electrons in metal wires drift in the opposite direction.

## Voltage is energy per charge

A potential difference of 1 volt means 1 joule of energy transferred per coulomb of charge. A battery does not simply “store current”; it maintains a potential difference by chemical processes.

## Resistance and Ohm's law

For an ohmic component at approximately fixed physical conditions, (V=IR). Resistance (R) is measured in ohms. This relationship is not universal for every device: diodes, lamps at changing temperature, and many materials are nonlinear.

Electrical power can be written (P=IV=I^2R=V^2/R) for an ohmic resistor under the relevant conditions.

## Series and parallel

- **Series:** same current through each component; voltage drops add.
- **Parallel:** same voltage across each branch; branch currents add.

For resistors:
- Series: (R_{\text{eq}}=R_1+R_2+\dots)
- Parallel: (1/R_{\text{eq}}=1/R_1+1/R_2+\dots)

## Kirchhoff's rules

At a junction, total current entering equals total current leaving (charge conservation). Around a closed loop, signed voltage rises and drops sum to zero (energy conservation).

## Safety

Mains electricity can cause serious injury or fire. Never experiment with household wall outlets. Learn circuit principles with low-voltage, current-limited educational equipment and adult/qualified supervision where appropriate.
