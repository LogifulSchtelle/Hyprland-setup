# 10 — Rotation, Torque, and Angular Momentum

## Rotation has its own motion vocabulary

Angular position describes orientation; angular velocity describes how quickly it changes; angular acceleration describes how quickly angular velocity changes. For circular motion, tangential speed is (v=r\omega), where \(\omega\) is angular speed in radians per second.

## Torque: the turning effect of force

Torque magnitude is (\tau=rF\sin\theta), where (r) is distance from pivot to the force application point. A force applied farther from a hinge can produce more turning effect. The perpendicular lever arm is what matters.

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis, (\sum\tau=I\alpha), the rotational analogue of Newton's second law. Moment of inertia (I) measures how strongly the mass distribution resists angular acceleration; it depends on where the mass sits relative to the axis.

## Angular momentum

For a simple rigid body about a fixed axis, (L=I\omega). When external torque on a system is negligible, angular momentum is conserved. A spinning skater rotates faster when drawing mass inward because the moment of inertia decreases.

## Rolling

For rolling without slipping, (v_{\text{cm}}=R\omega). The object has both translational and rotational kinetic energy. Its total kinetic energy is (K=\frac12Mv_{\text{cm}}^2+\frac12I\omega^2).

## Equilibrium

Static equilibrium requires both net force and net torque to be zero. Zero net force alone does not prevent an object from beginning to rotate.
