# 04 — Motion Graphs and Constant Acceleration

Graphs are not decoration. Their slopes and areas encode physical meaning.

## Position–time graph

The slope is velocity: a steep slope means a large velocity magnitude; a flat segment means position is not changing. A negative slope means motion in the negative direction.

## Velocity–time graph

The slope is acceleration. The signed area under the graph is displacement. If velocity stays at 4 m/s for 3 s, the area is (4\times3=12\) m of displacement.

If the graph crosses zero velocity, the object changes direction. For total distance, add the magnitudes of the areas for each segment rather than allowing opposite directions to cancel.

## Acceleration–time graph

The area under an acceleration–time graph is the change in velocity, because acceleration is change in velocity per time.

## Constant acceleration equations

For constant (a), average velocity equals ((v_0+v)/2), so displacement is (\Delta x=\frac{v_0+v}{2}t). Combining this with (v=v_0+at) gives the standard equations from the previous lesson.

## Free fall

Near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance, objects share approximately the same gravitational acceleration (g\approx9.81\,\text{m/s}^2) downward. A heavy object does not fall faster solely because it is heavier. Air resistance can change real-world results, especially for objects with large area relative to mass.
