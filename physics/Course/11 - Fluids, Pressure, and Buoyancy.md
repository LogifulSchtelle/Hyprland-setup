# 11 — Fluids, Pressure, and Buoyancy

## Pressure describes distributed force

Pressure is normal force per area: (P=F_\perp/A). Its SI unit is the pascal (Pa), equal to N/m². The same force concentrated on a smaller area creates greater pressure.

## Pressure in a fluid

For a stationary fluid of constant density, pressure increases with depth:
(P=P_0+\rho gh),
where (P_0) is surface pressure, \(\rho\) density, (g) gravitational acceleration and (h) depth. At the same depth in the same connected fluid, pressure is the same regardless of container shape.

## Buoyancy

A submerged object displaces fluid. Pressure is greater on its lower surfaces than upper surfaces, producing an upward buoyant force. Archimedes' principle states that buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid: (F_b=\rho_{\text{fluid}}gV_{\text{displaced}}).

An object floats when it can displace enough fluid for buoyancy to balance its weight. A steel ship floats because its overall average density, including hollow spaces, is less than water's density.

## Flow

For steady incompressible flow through a pipe, (A_1v_1=A_2v_2): narrower sections have greater flow speed if the volume flow rate is constant. Bernoulli's equation relates pressure, flow speed and height for idealized steady flow along a streamline. Real fluids have viscosity and energy losses, so the ideal relation has limits.
