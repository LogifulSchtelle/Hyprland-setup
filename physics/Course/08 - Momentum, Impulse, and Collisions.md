# 08 — Momentum, Impulse, and Collisions

## Momentum describes motion in a useful way

Classical momentum is (\vec p=m\vec v). It is a vector, so direction matters. A slowly moving truck can have more momentum than a fast tennis ball because its mass is much larger.

## Impulse changes momentum

Impulse is force accumulated over time: (\vec J=\vec F_{\text{avg}}\Delta t=\Delta\vec p). A force can produce the same momentum change over a long time with a smaller average force. This is why airbags and padded surfaces increase stopping time.

## Conservation of momentum

For a system with negligible net external impulse, total momentum stays constant. Objects may exchange momentum internally, but those interactions do not change the system total.

For two objects in one dimension:
(m_1v_{1i}+m_2v_{2i}=m_1v_{1f}+m_2v_{2f}).

## Collision types

- **Elastic:** total kinetic energy and momentum are conserved.
- **Inelastic:** momentum is conserved for an isolated system, but kinetic energy is not.
- **Perfectly inelastic:** objects stick together after collision.

Kinetic energy not retained as kinetic energy becomes other forms such as deformation, sound, and thermal energy. Total energy still obeys conservation.

## Example: objects stick together

A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg cart and they stick. With negligible external impulse, final velocity is (v_f=(2\cdot3+1\cdot0)/(2+1)=2\) m/s.
