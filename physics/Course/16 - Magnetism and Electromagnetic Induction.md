# 16 — Magnetism and Electromagnetic Induction

## Magnetic fields
Magnets and moving electric charges produce magnetic fields. A charge moving with velocity \(\vec v\) through a field \(\vec B\) feels \(\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B\), with magnitude \(F=|q|vB\sin\theta\). The force is perpendicular to both velocity and field, so a magnetic force alone changes the direction of motion, not the particle's kinetic energy.

## Current creates magnetism
A current in a wire creates a magnetic field around it. Coiling the wire strengthens and organizes the field; an iron core can increase it further. This is the principle behind electromagnets, relays, speakers and many motors.

## Induction
A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force (emf), a voltage-like quantity. Faraday's law describes its magnitude. Lenz's law says the induced effect opposes the *change in flux* that caused it. That opposition follows from energy conservation; it does not mean the induced field always points opposite the original field.

## Generators and transformers
A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy by changing magnetic flux. A transformer transfers energy between coils through changing magnetic flux, so it needs changing current rather than steady DC after the initial transient. For an ideal transformer, \(V_s/V_p=N_s/N_p\), where \(N\) is coil turns.

## Electromagnetic waves
Changing electric and magnetic fields can propagate through vacuum. Radio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are all electromagnetic waves, differing by frequency and wavelength.
