# 06 — Forces: Friction, Tension, Springs, and Normal Force

## Contact forces come from microscopic interactions

Surfaces resist interpenetration through electromagnetic interactions between atoms. At ordinary scales, we represent the combined effect as contact forces.

- **Normal force:** contact force perpendicular to a surface.
- **Tension:** pulling force transmitted through a taut cord or cable.
- **Friction:** force parallel to surfaces that opposes relative sliding or the tendency to slide.
- **Spring force:** restoring force produced by deformation.

## Friction

Static friction adjusts as needed up to a maximum: (f_s\leq\mu_sN). It is not always equal to (\mu_sN). Kinetic friction during sliding is often approximated by (f_k=\mu_kN). Here (N) is the normal-force magnitude and (\mu) is a dimensionless friction coefficient. These are useful empirical models, not universal microscopic laws.

## Springs

For an ideal spring within its elastic range, Hooke's law is (F=-kx). The minus sign says the restoring force points opposite the displacement from equilibrium. The spring constant (k) measures stiffness in N/m.

## Inclines

Resolve weight into components parallel and perpendicular to a slope of angle \(\theta\):
- Parallel: (mg\sin\theta)
- Perpendicular: (mg\cos\theta)

If no other perpendicular forces act, the normal force is (N=mg\cos\theta). Friction then depends on the normal force, not automatically on the full weight (mg).

## Strategy

Draw the object, choose axes that simplify the geometry, label every force, resolve angled forces into components, then apply Newton's second law separately along each axis.
