# 22 — Astronomy and Cosmology

## Light is a time machine
Astronomers study electromagnetic radiation, cosmic rays, neutrinos and gravitational waves. Because light takes time to travel, distant objects are seen as they were when their light departed. We see the Sun about eight minutes in the past; distant galaxies can be seen billions of years in the past.

## Stars
Stars form as gravity compresses gas until the core becomes hot and dense. During much of a star's life, pressure balances gravitational compression. Nuclear fusion powers stars, while mass and available fuel shape their evolution. Many elements are created in stellar processes; some heavy nuclei form in explosive and neutron-rich astrophysical events.

## Galaxies and cosmic expansion
Galaxies contain stars, gas, dust and dark matter. Observations show that the universe expands on large scales, with distant galaxies generally exhibiting redshift consistent with this expansion. This is not simply an explosion from one special point into pre-existing empty space.

## The Big Bang model
The Big Bang model describes the observable universe evolving from an early hot, dense state. Major evidence includes cosmic expansion, the cosmic microwave background and the abundance of light elements. It does not, by itself, tell us with certainty what preceded the earliest state our current theories can describe.

## Dark components
Galactic motion, gravitational lensing and cosmological observations indicate that visible matter alone cannot explain all gravitational effects. **Dark matter** is the name for an inferred gravitating component whose nature remains unknown. **Dark energy** describes accelerated cosmic expansion in the simplest cosmological model; its underlying nature remains uncertain.
